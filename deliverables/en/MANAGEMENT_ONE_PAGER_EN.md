# Decision Brief: AI Transformation at Darmstädter and Klevers

## Current situation

Darmstädter and Klevers possess a valuable body of accumulated know-how and data: materials, textile structures, coatings, laminations, formulations, process settings, laboratory and quality results, certifications, complaints, supplier information, and expert experience.

Today, much of this information is distributed across systems, files, reports, and individual knowledge. As a result, relationships between order, roll, material, process, inspection, test result, defect, and customer outcome are difficult and time-consuming to reconstruct.

## Strategic opportunity

The largest AI opportunity is not immediate machine automation. It is the secure connection of technical knowledge, process data, and quality evidence into a continuously improving industrial decision system.

Over time, this can become a proprietary Materials and Quality Intelligence Platform that:

- identifies repeated quality problems earlier;
- accelerates root-cause analysis;
- reduces scrap and rework;
- preserves expert knowledge;
- supports development and scale-up;
- improves material and formulation decisions;
- answers technical customer questions faster with evidence.

## Recommended first step

### Two-week discovery sprint

Purpose: validate data availability, linkability, value, risk, and the exact pilot scope using real internal information.

Questions:

- Which INTEX data can be exported read-only?
- Which ELSIS data, images, defect labels, timestamps, and roll/order references are available?
- How are QC and laboratory results stored?
- Which historical process parameters exist?
- Can order, roll, process, inspection, QC, rework, and complaint records be linked reliably?
- What are the current costs of scrap, rework, quality holds, and complaint investigation?
- Which single line and product family provide the strongest starting point?

Outputs:

- data-source and ownership map;
- data-quality and linkage assessment;
- quality-cost baseline;
- selected line and product family;
- final pilot architecture;
- credible budget and value range;
- go/revise/stop recommendation.

## Proposed pilot after successful discovery

### Roll Quality Intelligence Pilot

Duration: 12 weeks  
Scope: one line, one product family, 6–12 months of history  
Operating risk: low; read-only access, no machine control, and no ERP writeback.

Deliverables:

1. roll-level quality passport;
2. order/roll/process/inspection/QC linkage;
3. controlled defect taxonomy;
4. defect Pareto and recurrence analysis;
5. faster, source-linked root-cause evidence retrieval;
6. management dashboard;
7. quantified improvement opportunities;
8. decision package for predictive quality and possible CV expansion.

## Security and governance principles

- Read-only first: observe and explain before recommending or controlling.
- Human accountability: no automatic release, certification, customer communication, or process change.
- Evidence requirement: technical AI answers must show traceable sources and uncertainty.
- IP protection: formulations, process windows, customer data, and internal know-how must not be uploaded to uncontrolled public AI services.
- Privacy and employee trust: no hidden performance scoring or surveillance.
- Stage-gated investment: no large platform investment before measurable value is demonstrated.

## Decision requested

Approve the two-week discovery sprint and nominate:

- one executive sponsor;
- one AI transformation lead;
- one responsible representative from R&D, Production, Quality, Packaging/Inspection, IT/Security, and Finance/Controlling;
- the relevant data owners.

The larger 12-week pilot decision will be made only after the internal data, risk, and ROI assessment is complete.

> The objective is not to turn Darmstädter and Klevers into “AI companies.” The objective is to strengthen technical-textile expertise, quality, and development speed through secure, measurable AI enablement.
