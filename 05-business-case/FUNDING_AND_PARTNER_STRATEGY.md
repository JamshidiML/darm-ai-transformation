# Funding and Research Partner Strategy

## 1. Principle

Funding should accelerate a validated industrial R&D program; it should not delay discovery or be used to justify a weak use case. Begin the two-week discovery sprint with internal approval. Use the resulting data, technical uncertainty, baseline, and pilot evidence to select the correct funding route.

All program rules, eligibility, budgets, deadlines, and state-aid conditions must be revalidated immediately before application.

## 2. Workstream-to-funding fit

| Workstream | Best-fit route | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Internal data discovery and dashboard | Internal budget | Fast, bounded, primarily operational discovery |
| Predictive quality with genuine technical uncertainty | Forschungszulage and/or ZIM | Experimental R&D with uncertain technical outcome |
| Materials/formulation intelligence | ZIM, Forschungszulage, university/Fraunhofer cooperation | Strong industrial R&D and knowledge-creation fit |
| Physics-informed scale-up model | ZIM, Eurostars, research partnership | Research-heavy, defensible technical uncertainty |
| New marketable industrial AI product | NRW innovation instruments, Eurostars, EIC later | Requires external commercialization logic |
| Energy/process optimization | BAFA EEW or relevant efficiency programs | Fit depends on eligible energy/resource-saving investment |
| AI/data engineer hiring | Relevant NRW innovation-assistant route if eligible | Capability building |
| Cybersecurity for the platform | Relevant digital-security support if eligible | Security-specific investment |
| Large-scale platform after proof | Innovation loans / internal investment | Scale-up after ROI evidence |

## 3. Recommended sequence

### Phase A — Internal discovery

Produce:

- data map;
- baseline and addressable quality cost;
- technical uncertainty statement;
- pilot scope;
- architecture;
- partner need assessment;
- expected commercial/industrial impact.

### Phase B — Internally approved pilot

Generate evidence:

- linkage and data-quality results;
- first quality passport;
- repeated-defect findings;
- quantified opportunities;
- first technical research questions.

### Phase C — R&D funding package

Separate operational implementation from research.

Operational package:

- data integration;
- dashboard;
- workflow;
- governance;
- adoption.

R&D package:

- predictive quality under changing product/process conditions;
- formulation and material interaction modeling;
- uncertainty-aware experiment recommendation;
- scale-up prediction from laboratory to production;
- supplier/material substitution with performance constraints;
- physics-informed models for coating, curing, heat transfer, or quality behavior.

## 4. Technical uncertainty template

A funding-ready R&D project should answer:

```text
Technical objective:
Current state of the art:
Why existing methods are insufficient:
Scientific/technical uncertainties:
Experiments and model-development work required:
Failure risk:
Expected new knowledge:
Industrial application:
Commercial or strategic impact:
```

Avoid framing ordinary software integration as R&D.

## 5. Potential partner roles

| Partner type | Possible contribution |
|---|---|
| RWTH Aachen ITA | Technical-textile research, weaving/material expertise, validation, consortium credibility |
| Fraunhofer institute | Applied AI, manufacturing, materials, process modeling, validation, funding support |
| University data/AI group | Statistical learning, uncertainty, active learning, knowledge graphs, explainability |
| Machine/inspection vendor | Data interface, signal interpretation, export, system constraints |
| Chemical/raw-material supplier | Material data, formulation insight, alternative grades, joint validation |
| Customer/application partner | Requirements, field validation, commercialization evidence |
| Specialized AI integrator | Secure data engineering, MLOps, applications—under company data/IP controls |

Partner selection must follow the architecture, security, data ownership, and IP rules in this repository.

## 6. Partner selection scorecard

Score 1–5.

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Relevant industrial/technical-textile expertise | 20% |
| Ability to work with messy industrial data | 15% |
| Security and confidential-IP maturity | 15% |
| Technical depth in required R&D area | 15% |
| Ownership/export rights and low lock-in | 10% |
| Funding and consortium experience | 10% |
| Ability to transfer knowledge internally | 10% |
| Cost and commercial transparency | 5% |

## 7. IP and contract requirements

Before partner work begins, define:

- background IP owned before the project;
- foreground IP created in the project;
- rights to data, schemas, ontology, knowledge graph, features, code, models, prompts, evaluation sets, and documentation;
- publication and confidentiality review;
- patent/invention process;
- reuse restrictions;
- data/model deletion and return;
- export and transition assistance;
- subcontractor/subprocessor approval.

## 8. Funding readiness checklist

- executive sponsor;
- approved project owner;
- clear industrial problem;
- measurable baseline;
- genuine technical uncertainty;
- work packages and milestones;
- experimental plan;
- budget and internal capacity;
- partner roles;
- exploitation/commercialization plan;
- risks;
- data/IP/security plan;
- evidence that the company can implement results.

## 9. Immediate actions

1. Do not submit a funding application before discovery identifies the strongest technical R&D question.
2. During discovery, document every uncertainty that cannot be solved through routine engineering.
3. Create a shortlist of research partners only after pilot scope is frozen.
4. Prepare separate narratives for internal transformation and external marketable product potential.
5. Use the pilot as evidence for a larger materials-intelligence R&D program.
