# Darmstädter + Klevers Corporate AI Transformation Strategy

**Version:** 1.0  
**Audience:** Management, R&D, Production, Quality, IT, Finance  
**Status:** Management discussion draft  

## Executive thesis

Darmstädter and Klevers create value through engineered combinations of materials, textile structures, coatings, finishes, laminations, process windows, laboratory evidence, certifications, and application knowledge. The strategic AI opportunity is therefore not generic automation. It is the conversion of this accumulated know-how into a secure, traceable, and continuously improving industrial decision system.

The recommended path is staged:

1. approve a two-week discovery sprint;
2. validate data feasibility, quality costs, ownership, and pilot scope;
3. run a bounded 12-week read-only quality-intelligence pilot on one line and one product family;
4. scale only after measurable value and operational feasibility are demonstrated.

## Why now

The companies possess valuable but fragmented information across ERP, quality records, laboratory files, inspection systems, production settings, operator experience, customer requirements, certifications, complaints, supplier documents, and historical development projects.

When these sources are disconnected:

- the same problems may be investigated repeatedly;
- expert knowledge remains dependent on individuals;
- process and quality relationships are difficult to prove;
- complaint evidence is slow to assemble;
- R&D experiments and failures are under-reused;
- data generated by inspection and production systems may not become organizational learning.

A controlled AI transformation can convert these weaknesses into a defensible advantage.

## Strategic north star

Create an Industrial Materials and Quality Intelligence Platform that can eventually:

- retrieve source-linked technical knowledge;
- connect each roll to order, material, process, inspection, QC, laboratory, rework, shipment, and complaint evidence;
- identify repeated defects and likely contributing factors;
- preserve expert rules and troubleshooting knowledge;
- recommend relevant historical experiments and comparable products;
- support formulation, scale-up, supplier, and material-substitution decisions;
- predict quality risk and propose human-approved actions;
- improve customer response, certification evidence, and technical sales speed.

## Priority value pools

### 1. Quality intelligence

Near-term outcomes:

- roll-level quality passport;
- defect Pareto by product, process, line, and time;
- repeated-defect analysis;
- faster complaint and root-cause evidence retrieval;
- improved traceability and release visibility.

Long-term outcomes:

- predictive quality;
- supplier-lot quality intelligence;
- automated evidence packets and CAPA support;
- prevention rather than late inspection.

### 2. R&D and materials intelligence

Near-term outcomes:

- structured historical experiment memory;
- source-cited search across reports, TDS, SDS, certificates, specifications, and test records;
- digital capture of new experiments and lessons learned;
- retrieval of similar projects, failures, and successful combinations.

Long-term outcomes:

- formulation and process-window recommendation;
- experiment planning and Bayesian optimization;
- scale-up prediction;
- raw-material substitution intelligence;
- physics-informed materials and process models.

### 3. Production intelligence

Near-term outcomes:

- parameter-to-quality analysis;
- anomaly and process-drift visibility;
- standardized operator observations;
- changeover and bottleneck transparency.

Long-term outcomes:

- human-approved process recommendations;
- predictive maintenance;
- scheduling optimization;
- digital twins for critical processes.

### 4. Knowledge and compliance intelligence

Near-term outcomes:

- approved-document assistant with strict source references;
- faster certificate, TDS, SDS, standard, and customer-requirement retrieval;
- complaint and prior-case search;
- expert knowledge capture.

Long-term outcomes:

- certificate-aware product design;
- controlled technical content generation;
- regulatory and supplier risk monitoring.

## First operational program

### Gate 1 — Two-week discovery sprint

Questions to answer:

- Can INTEX data be exported read-only, and which identifiers are available?
- What ELSIS data, images, labels, timestamps, and roll/order identifiers exist?
- How are QC and laboratory results stored and linked?
- Which process parameters are historically available?
- Can roll, order, process, inspection, QC, rework, and complaint records be connected?
- What are the current annual costs of scrap, rework, repeated defects, quality holds, and complaint investigation?
- Which one line and one product family offer the best combination of pain, value, volume, and data readiness?

Deliverable: a management-ready pilot business case with final scope, cost, architecture, risks, and expected value.

### Gate 2 — Twelve-week read-only pilot

Working title: **Roll Quality Intelligence Pilot**

Scope:

- one line;
- one product family;
- 6–12 months of historical data where available;
- no machine control or ERP writeback.

Pilot outputs:

1. canonical roll-level data model;
2. data linkage and data quality report;
3. roll quality passport prototype;
4. defect taxonomy and defect Pareto;
5. repeated-defect and root-cause evidence analysis;
6. management dashboard;
7. quantified improvement opportunities;
8. recommendation on predictive quality and computer-vision expansion.

## Decision principles

- No AI project without a business owner, data owner, KPI, and decision workflow.
- No technical answer without identifiable evidence and uncertainty.
- No customer, certification, quality-release, or process change without human approval.
- No confidential data in unapproved public AI systems.
- No employee-performance scoring in the pilot.
- No platform-scale investment before data and value are proven.
- Prefer open standards, exportable data, and modular architecture.

## Program roadmap

### 0–3 months

- management alignment and governance;
- data discovery;
- pilot selection and baseline;
- secure read-only data environment;
- first quality passport and knowledge-search prototypes.

### 3–6 months

- complete quality-intelligence pilot;
- standardize identifiers and defect taxonomy;
- quantify improvement opportunities;
- build first predictive baseline only if data quality supports it;
- capture operator and R&D knowledge.

### 6–12 months

- expand to selected additional product families;
- establish digital lab notebook and R&D knowledge graph;
- add supplier-document and material intelligence;
- introduce model monitoring, governance, and repeatable data pipelines.

### 1–3 years

- predictive quality across major product families;
- formulation and scale-up support;
- application-engineering and technical-sales assistance;
- supplier and complaint intelligence;
- scheduling, maintenance, and energy optimization where data supports value.

### 3–5 years

- proprietary materials intelligence platform;
- digital twins for selected critical processes;
- human-approved closed-loop recommendations;
- AI-enabled customer co-development;
- patentable data-driven formulations or robust process windows where appropriate.

## Required management decisions

Approve the discovery gate and nominate:

- one executive sponsor;
- one AI transformation lead;
- one owner each from R&D, production, quality, packaging/inspection, IT/security, and finance;
- controlled access to the selected systems and documents;
- a clear employee-communication and privacy process.

The program should be judged on measurable quality, cost, time, knowledge, risk, and customer outcomes—not on the number of AI tools deployed.
