# Darm AI Transformation Program Charter

**Program:** Darmstädter + Klevers AI Transformation  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Proposed for management alignment  
**Program owner:** To be appointed  
**AI transformation lead:** Mohsen Jamshidi (proposed)  
**Initial horizon:** Discovery gate + 12-week pilot  

## 1. Purpose

The program converts fragmented technical, process, quality, laboratory, certification, supplier, and expert knowledge into a secure and measurable industrial intelligence capability.

The program is not a general automation initiative and is not a proposal for uncontrolled use of generative AI. It begins with a bounded, read-only quality-intelligence pilot and scales only after value, feasibility, security, and employee acceptance are demonstrated.

## 2. Strategic objective

Build a durable data and knowledge foundation that enables Darmstädter and Klevers to:

- reduce scrap, rework, repeated defects, and complaint-investigation time;
- improve roll-level traceability and quality evidence;
- preserve expert knowledge and historical R&D learning;
- accelerate product development and material/formulation decisions;
- improve production repeatability without removing human authority;
- create a proprietary industrial AI capability that competitors cannot easily copy.

## 3. Phase-gate model

### Gate 0 — Management alignment

Approve:

- one executive sponsor;
- a cross-functional discovery team;
- controlled read-only access to selected systems and documents;
- a two-week discovery sprint;
- confidentiality, security, and employee-communication principles.

### Gate 1 — Data discovery and business case

Duration: 2 weeks.

Deliverables:

- data-source inventory and ownership map;
- INTEX, ELSIS, QC, laboratory, production, and complaint feasibility assessment;
- roll/order/process/QC linkage score;
- initial quality-cost baseline;
- selected line and product family;
- final 12-week pilot scope, architecture, budget, risks, and expected value.

Decision: go, revise, or stop.

### Gate 2 — Quality Intelligence pilot

Duration: 12 weeks after Gate 1 approval.

Deliverables:

- roll-level quality passport prototype;
- defect Pareto and repeated-defect analysis;
- evidence-linked root-cause retrieval;
- data-quality report;
- baseline dashboard and quantified improvement opportunities;
- recommendation on predictive quality and computer-vision expansion.

### Gate 3 — Scale decision

Scale only when the pilot demonstrates measurable value, usable data, acceptable risk, and clear ownership.

## 4. Initial scope

The initial operational scope is intentionally narrow:

- one coating or finishing line;
- one high-volume or high-pain product family;
- one defined historical period, preferably 6–12 months;
- order/roll context, available process parameters, inspection results, QC/lab results, operator notes, rework, scrap, and complaints;
- read-only analysis only.

## 5. Explicitly out of scope for the first phase

- autonomous machine control;
- automatic ERP writeback;
- automatic customer communication;
- automatic release, certification, or product approval;
- unsupervised formulation recommendations;
- employee-performance scoring or surveillance;
- uploading confidential formulations or customer data to unapproved public AI services.

## 6. Success criteria

The program is successful at the first scale decision when it can demonstrate:

1. a reliable linkage rate across the selected roll/order/process/QC evidence;
2. an agreed defect taxonomy and data ownership model;
3. identification of the most important defect and quality-cost drivers;
4. faster retrieval of comparable historical cases and evidence;
5. a quantified improvement opportunity with an auditable financial model;
6. management, IT, quality, production, R&D, and employee acceptance of the proposed next phase.

## 7. Operating principles

1. **Business value before technology.** Every use case must connect to a measurable decision, risk, cost, time, quality, or customer outcome.
2. **Read-only first.** Observe and explain before recommending; recommend before controlling.
3. **Human accountability.** People remain responsible for technical, quality, safety, legal, and customer decisions.
4. **Source-linked answers.** Technical outputs must identify their evidence and uncertainty.
5. **Security by design.** Access control, data minimization, auditability, and IP protection are mandatory.
6. **No shadow AI.** Approved architecture, tools, and data flows are required.
7. **Operator participation.** Production knowledge is a core asset; operators are contributors, not monitoring targets.
8. **Stage-gated investment.** No platform-scale investment before pilot evidence.

## 8. Governance cadence

- Weekly pilot working session: 30–45 minutes.
- Biweekly sponsor update: decisions, blockers, risks, and value evidence.
- Monthly one-page management report: KPI status, spending, findings, next decision.
- Formal gate review at the end of discovery and pilot.

## 9. Required roles

- Executive sponsor
- AI transformation lead
- R&D owner
- Production owner
- Quality owner
- Packaging/inspection owner
- IT/security owner
- Finance/controlling owner
- Data owners for each source system
- Employee representative / works council contact where required
